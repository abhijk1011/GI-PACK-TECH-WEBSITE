## Where the water comes from

A sealed steel container can arrive with water dripping from the roof, rust on machined surfaces and caked powder at the top of the load — without ever having leaked. The water was already inside.

Air holds more moisture when it is warm. A container loaded in a warm, humid place carries that moisture with it, and the cargo adds more: wooden pallets and dunnage, cartons, and agricultural products that give off moisture as they respire. When the air inside cools below its **dew point**, the moisture condenses.

## Container sweat and cargo sweat

| | Container sweat (container rain) | Cargo sweat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where it forms | On the inside of the container's walls and roof | On the surface of the cargo |
| When | The outside cools — night, colder sea areas, winter ports | Cold cargo meets warm, humid air |
| Typical damage | Drips onto the top of the load: wet cartons, caked powder, rust | Wet machined surfaces, rust, mould, label damage |
| Typical route | Warm and humid origin, cooler route or destination | Cold origin, warm and humid destination |

Both can happen on the same voyage. Day-night cycling can repeat the process many times.

## The controls, from the container to the product

**At container level**, ventilation, dry pallets and container desiccants reduce the amount of moisture in the air. They help, but they act on the whole container's air, which the cargo keeps replenishing.

**At product level**, the reliable control is to put the goods that must stay dry inside their own sealed environment, so it does not matter what the container's air is doing.

## Machinery and equipment

For a machine tool, generator, panel or compressor, the standard protection is a **sealed barrier enclosure**:

1. An [aluminium vacuum laminate cover](/products/machine-covers-and-protective-packaging/aluminium-vacuum-laminate-cover) — close to a total barrier to moisture and gas — fabricated to the machine and heat sealed around it, often on a sealed base sheet.
2. **Desiccant** inside, calculated against the enclosed volume, the barrier and the transit time, to absorb the moisture trapped at sealing.
3. **VCI** protection for bare metal — see [what is VCI packaging](/blog/what-is-vci-packaging).
4. Optionally, the air is **drawn down by vacuum** before sealing, which removes most of the moisture with it.

The container can sweat around the enclosure; the air next to the machine stays dry. The [seaworthy export packing guide](/blog/seaworthy-export-packing-for-machinery) sets out the full method.

## Powders and chemicals

For hygroscopic powders, the protection is a barrier liner inside the drum or FIBC — an [aluminium multilayer liner](/products/bulk-bag-and-fibc-liners/multilayer-aluminium-laminated-liner-for-powders) — sealed after filling. Container rain landing on the top of an FIBC then wets the woven bag, not the product.

## Pallets of cartons and parts

- A [pallet top sheet](/products/pallet-covers-and-pallet-wraps/pallet-top-sheet-and-liner) closes the open top that stretch wrap leaves — exactly where drips land — and a pallet liner protects the bottom layer from the pallet itself.
- A [VCI pallet cover](/products/pallet-covers-and-pallet-wraps/vci-pallet-cover) protects a whole pallet of metal parts against corrosion in one operation.

## Bulk agricultural cargo

Respiring cargo — grain, coffee, cocoa, seeds — must not be sealed airtight, or it will sweat inside its own packaging. An **unlaminated, breathable** [container liner](/products/container-liners-and-sacks/container-liner-pp-woven-and-pe-film) lets heat and moisture escape while still keeping the cargo off the container walls. See [container liners for dry bulk](/blog/container-liners-for-dry-bulk).

## A checklist for a moisture-safe shipment

- Know the route: where it is warm and humid, where it is cold, how long it takes.
- Load dry: kiln-dried pallets and dunnage, cargo at a stable temperature.
- Seal what must stay dry inside its own barrier, with enough desiccant.
- Protect bare metal with VCI rather than relying on dryness alone.
- Keep respiring cargo in breathable packaging.
- Open containers at destination in a way that limits warm, humid air rushing over cold cargo.
