## Why India, and why a manufacturer

Industrial flexible packaging — drum liners, FIBC liners, container liners, heavy duty sacks, machine covers — is rarely bought off a shelf. The useful products are made to a particular drum, a particular powder or a particular machine, and that is exactly what makes the choice of supplier matter.

India has one of the world's largest bases of polyethylene film, woven polypropylene and lamination capacity, and a long history of exporting FIBCs and liners. What separates suppliers is not whether they can quote a low price for a standard size. It is whether they can make *your* size, in *your* material, and prove it with a sample before the order.

That is the difference between a **manufacturer** and a **trader**. A trader buys from several plants and quotes from a list. A manufacturer runs the extrusion, lamination, cutting and sealing lines itself, so a non-standard diameter or an unusual film structure is a production question rather than a sourcing one.

## How to qualify an Indian packaging supplier

Use the same checklist whichever supplier you are assessing.

| What to check | What a good answer looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Processes in-house | Extrusion, lamination, cutting, fabrication and heat sealing on its own lines |
| Quality system | A current certificate such as ISO 9001:2015, available with the quotation |
| Material knowledge | Can explain what each layer in a barrier film does, and when a material is the wrong answer |

## What to send for a firm quotation

Made-to-order packaging has no list price, so "please send your price list" gets you either nothing useful or a number that changes later. Send a specification instead:

1. **The product being packed**, with its data sheet — this decides material compatibility, barrier and static requirements.
2. **The container or machine** — measured internal diameter and height of the drum, the FIBC dimensions, the container size, or the machine's general arrangement drawing.
3. **How it is filled, handled and discharged** — gravity or pneumatic filling, forklift or crane, how the product comes out.
4. **Special requirements** — antistatic or conductive, oxygen or moisture barrier, food contact, UV exposure, temperature.
5. **Quantity** — the approximate annual volume and the first order size.
6. **Destination and terms** — the destination port or delivery address and the Incoterm you buy on.

With those six, a manufacturer can specify the construction and quote it in one round. GI PackTech's [specification form](/specify) asks exactly these questions.

## Incoterms, ports and documents

Agree the commercial frame at quotation stage, not at shipment.

- **Incoterm.** Packaging is commonly bought on FOB (free on board at the Indian port), CIF or CFR (to your port), or FCA and DAP where it travels by air or door to door. Say which you use and the quotation is made on that basis.
- **Port.** Valsad lies on the Mumbai–Ahmedabad industrial corridor, within road distance of Nhava Sheva (JNPT), one of India's main container ports, and Hazira. The port is confirmed with the quotation.
- **Documents.** For any import you will need at least a commercial invoice and packing list, and usually a certificate of origin and the bill of lading or air waybill. Where your market needs material documentation — food-contact declarations, test reports, a quality certificate — ask for it before ordering so the right grade is specified from the start.

## Customs classification

Classification depends on the material and the form of the product, and your customs broker has the final word. As a starting point, the common headings in the Harmonized System are:

| Product | Usual HS heading |
| --- | --- |
| Bags and liners of polyethylene film (drum liners, FIBC liners, sacks) | 3923.21 |
| Bags and liners of other plastics, such as PVC | 3923.29 |
| FIBC jumbo bags of woven polypropylene | 6305.32 |
| Other woven polypropylene or polyethylene sacks | 6305.33 |

Machine covers, container liners and composite or aluminium-laminate products can fall under different headings depending on their construction, so confirm each line item with your broker before the first shipment.

## Samples, trials and the first order

A good first order follows the same sequence every time:

1. **Specification** — agreed in writing, including material structure, dimensions and any printing.
2. **Sample** — made to your dimensions and sent for trial fitment on your own drum, filling line or machine.
3. **Approval** — fit, seal, handling and, where relevant, barrier or static performance confirmed on your side.
4. **Bulk production** — made to the approved sample, with the same specification referenced on every repeat order.

Lead time depends on whether the film is one the plant already runs or has to be extruded or laminated for you, on the quantity, and on whether tooling is needed for a non-standard profile. A repeat order in an established structure is quickest; a new laminate takes longest because the film is made first. The date is confirmed with the quotation.
