## Where a lubricant drum loses money

A lubricant or base oil operation running steel or plastic drums is losing money in three places, and usually only one of them is on anybody's report:

1. **Residue.** Oil clings to the drum wall and the base. Every drum leaves product behind that was paid for, blended and filled.
2. **The drum itself.** Once product has touched the wall, the drum has to be cleaned, reconditioned or sold as scrap. A drum that could have made many trips makes one.
3. **The batch.** A drum opened again and again for draws exposes the whole batch to air, moisture and dust every time. The product degrades a little with each opening.

A drum liner closes the first two gaps. A liner with an integrated dispensing spout closes all three.

## How a liner changes the drum economics

A liner sits inside the drum and holds the product, so the drum wall never touches it. When the drum is empty, the liner — and the residue clinging to it — comes out, and the drum is clean.

That changes what a drum is. Instead of a one-trip container that is scrapped or reconditioned, it becomes a **returnable asset** that travels from plant to distributor and back on a regular route. For operations with sustainability targets or extended producer responsibility obligations, that is the difference between disposing of a drum and reusing one.

A liner made to the **measured** drum matters here. A liner that is too large folds and creases, and product gets trapped in the folds. One that is too small is stressed at the seams under the weight of the oil. GI PackTech makes liners to the drum's measured internal diameter and height, in any diameter from 6 to 100 inches — see [how to measure a drum for a liner](/blog/how-to-measure-a-drum-for-a-liner).

## The integrated dispensing spout

With an ordinary liner, the drum still has to be opened every time product is drawn. With a [drum liner with an integrated dispensing spout](/products/round-drum-liners/drum-liner-with-integrated-dispensing-spout), the operator draws only what is needed through the spout and the rest of the product stays sealed.

| | No liner | Plain liner | Liner with integrated spout |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product touches the drum wall | Yes | No | No |
| Drum reusable without cleaning | No | Yes | Yes |
| Residue left in the drum | High | Low | Low |
| Batch exposed on every draw | Yes | Yes | No |
| Controlled, measured dispensing | No | No | Yes |

### Why the spout seal matters

The joint between the spout and the film is the weak point of any spouted liner. A spout fitted in the field, by whoever is holding the liner, is the usual source of leaks. GI PackTech welds the spout fitment under controlled conditions on its own line and tests it before dispatch, and has run the fitment across PE, EVOH, aluminium laminate and conductive films — so a spout does not mean giving up barrier or static protection.

Spout size, type and position, liner diameter and height, gusset and bottom shape are all specified per order to suit the existing drum fleet and filling line.

## Choosing the film

| Situation | Film |
| --- | --- |
| Most lubricants and base oils | Multilayer co-extruded polyethylene |
| Long storage where moisture or oxygen pick-up degrades the product | [Aluminium barrier liner](/products/round-drum-liners/aluminium-barrier-drum-liner) or EVOH structure |
| Solvent-containing products or flammable atmospheres | Conductive film with a defined grounding arrangement |
| Hot filling | Film specified against the fill temperature — send it with the enquiry |
