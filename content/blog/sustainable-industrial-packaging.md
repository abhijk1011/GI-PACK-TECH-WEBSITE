## Start with what the packaging protects

Industrial packaging is a small fraction of the value — and of the embodied energy — of what it protects. A tonne of specialty chemical, a pharmaceutical intermediate or a machine tool represents far more material and energy than the liner or cover around it.

So the first sustainability question is not "how do we use less packaging?" but **"how do we stop losing product?"** A caked batch, a contaminated drum, a corroded machine or a burst sack sends everything upstream of it to waste. Specifying the right protection — a [barrier liner](/blog/evoh-vs-aluminium-barrier-liners) where moisture is the threat, a sealed cover where corrosion is — is the largest single gain available.

## Reuse: let the rigid container live longer

The heaviest packaging in most plants is the rigid kind — steel drums, IBCs, concrete bunds. Flexible packaging can make those last.

- **Drum liners.** With a liner, the product never touches the drum wall, so the drum can be reused instead of scrapped or reconditioned. Only the liner is discarded. A [spouted drum liner](/products/round-drum-liners/drum-liner-with-integrated-dispensing-spout) also keeps the batch sealed between draws. For operations with sustainability targets or **extended producer responsibility** duties, this turns a throwaway drum into a returnable asset — see [drum liners for lubricants](/blog/drum-liners-for-lubricants-and-reusable-drums).
- **Reusable covers.** [Woven laminated](/products/pallet-covers-and-pallet-wraps/woven-laminated-pallet-cover) and [PVC-coated fabric covers](/products/pallet-covers-and-pallet-wraps/pvc-pallet-cover-and-reusable-equipment-cover) replace single-use film covers that are bought again every time and often end up as litter around a plant.
- **Flexible bunds and tanks.** A [spill bund](/products/hazardous-waste-containment/spill-containment-bund-and-drip-tray) or bladder tank that folds flat moves from site to site, where a concrete bund or fixed tank is built once and left behind.

## Less material: engineered films

Thickness is an outcome, not a starting point. A **co-extruded** film puts different polyethylene grades in different layers, giving more strength and puncture resistance from less material than a thicker single-layer film. Replacing an over-thick plain liner with a thinner engineered one is a straight reduction in material, with no loss of performance — see the [multilayer plastic liner](/products/bulk-bag-and-fibc-liners/multilayer-plastic-liner).

Shipping formats matter too. A [container liner](/products/container-liners-and-sacks/container-liner-pp-woven-and-pe-film) replaces hundreds of sacks, their pallets and wrap with one liner, and a [liquid flexi bag](/products/liquid-flexi-bags-and-bladder-tanks/liquid-flexi-bag) removes the empty return journeys of an IBC fleet.

## Recyclability: choose mono-material where you can

| Construction | Recyclability | Use it when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single-material polyethylene | Most straightforward | Contamination protection and strength are enough |
| Co-extruded polyethylene (all PE layers) | Treated as polyethylene | You need more strength from less material |
| EVOH co-extruded with PE | Small barrier layer in a PE structure | Oxygen is the threat |
| Aluminium foil laminate | Difficult — mixed materials | Moisture or gas would otherwise ruin the product |
| PVC | Separate stream; check locally | Only PVC is compatible with the product |

The honest position is that **multilayer aluminium laminates trade recyclability for a barrier no single material can give**. Use them where the product needs that barrier, and mono-material polyethylene everywhere else. Recyclability also depends on what the packaging held — liners from hazardous products must be handled as that product's rules require.

## Regulation is moving in the same direction

Producer responsibility for plastic packaging is spreading. In India, extended producer responsibility under the Plastic Waste Management Rules places obligations on producers, importers and brand owners. In the European Union, the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) is raising requirements on recyclability, reuse and minimisation in stages. Exporters to the EU should ask their customers which requirements apply to industrial and transport packaging on their route, and specify with that in mind.

## A practical order of priorities

1. **Protect the product** — the right barrier, static control or corrosion protection.
2. **Reuse what is rigid** — liners for drums, reusable covers, folding bunds.
3. **Use less material** — engineered co-extruded films instead of thicker plain ones.
4. **Choose mono-material** where no multilayer barrier is needed.
5. **Ship smarter** — container liners and flexi bags instead of sacks and returnable IBCs where the route suits.
