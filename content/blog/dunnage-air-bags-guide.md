## Why cargo arrives broken in an undamaged container

A container can be lifted, stacked, sailed and trucked without a scratch, arrive with its seal intact — and still be full of broken goods. The damage happened inside: pallets slid on braking, drums tipped in a turn, cartons were crushed when a load shifted in heavy seas.

Any empty space inside a container, truck or wagon is space the load can move into, and movement builds momentum. The answer is to remove the space.

## What a dunnage bag does

A [dunnage air bag](/products/pallet-covers-and-pallet-wraps/dunnage-air-bag) is an inflatable bag placed in a gap — between rows of pallets, between drums, or between the load and a side wall — and then inflated. It expands to fill the void and presses the load units together, so they behave as one block that cannot slide or tip.

Its construction is an outer of multi-ply paper or polyethylene for strength and puncture resistance, around a polyethylene inner bladder that holds the air.

## Sizing: one size fits nothing

The right bag size for the gap is what makes a dunnage bag work.

- **Too small:** the bag inflates fully and still does not fill the gap. The load keeps moving.
- **Too large:** the bag cannot inflate to its designed shape in the gap, is over-stressed and can burst — or it is over-inflated to compensate.

Measure three things:

1. The **width of the gap** to be filled
2. The **height** of the load face the bag presses against
3. The **length** of that face

The bag is sized so that, inflated, it fills the gap and covers enough of the load face to spread the pressure. GI PackTech sizes against the actual load plan rather than selling one general size — send the plan and photographs of a loaded container.

## Placing and inflating

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| Place bags between load units, at mid-height of the load face | Place a bag against the container doors |
| Make sure both faces are flat and free of sharp edges | Put a bag against nails, strapping buckles or sharp corners |
| Inflate to the pressure stated by the maker | Over-inflate to make an undersized bag fit |
| Check the load is tight before closing the doors | Rely on bags alone for very heavy units |
| Use a slip sheet or board where the face is uneven | Leave large voids at the door end unblocked |

## Where dunnage bags fit in load securing

Dunnage bags are one part of securing a load. Heavy machinery, steel and large single items still need **blocking, bracing and lashing** to fixed points. Dunnage bags fill the gaps between units so the whole load is tight. International guidance on packing cargo transport units — the IMO/ILO/UNECE Code of Practice for Packing of Cargo Transport Units (the CTU Code) — treats void filling and load securing as part of the same job.

## Keep the units themselves stable

A dunnage bag holds pallets against each other, but each pallet still has to hold together. A [pallet shrink hood](/products/pallet-covers-and-pallet-wraps/pallet-shrink-hood) turns a stack into a single solid block, and an [LDPE pallet hood](/products/pallet-covers-and-pallet-wraps/ldpe-pallet-cover-and-hood) closes the top face that stretch wrap leaves open — see [pallet covers vs stretch wrap vs shrink hoods](/blog/pallet-covers-vs-stretch-wrap-vs-shrink-hoods).
