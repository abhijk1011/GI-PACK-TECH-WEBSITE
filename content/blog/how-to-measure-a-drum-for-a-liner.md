## The three measurements

Most drum liners can be specified from three facts:

1. **Internal diameter** — measured across the inside of the top opening, at its widest. For a drum with a rolled rim, measure inside the rim.
2. **Internal height** — from the inside of the base to the top of the rim.
3. **Drum type** — open-top (removable lid) or tight-head (fixed lid with a bung), and the material: steel, plastic or fibre.

Measure the drums you actually use, not a catalogue drawing. A "200 litre drum" from one maker is not the same internal size as one from another, and the difference is enough to matter.

## Reference sizes

These are typical figures for common drums, useful for a first conversation but not a substitute for measuring:

| Drum | Typical internal diameter | Typical internal height |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200–210 litre steel drum (55 US gallon) | About 22.5–23 in (570–585 mm) | About 33 in (840 mm) |
| Smaller steel and plastic drums | Varies widely by maker | Varies widely by maker |
| Fibre drums | Varies by maker and capacity | Varies by maker and capacity |
| Open-top vessels, bins and tanks | Up to 100 in for a GI PackTech liner | Any height |

## How the measurements become a liner

A tube liner is made from a flattened tube of film. Its **lay-flat width** is roughly half the drum's internal circumference (π × diameter ÷ 2) plus a small allowance, so it sits against the wall without stress. Its **length** covers the internal height and the base, plus an **overhang** that folds over the rim and is held by the lid or a band.

Get either dimension wrong and the liner tells you within one batch:

- **Too large:** the liner folds and creases, and product is trapped in the creases where it cannot be recovered.
- **Too small:** the seams carry the weight of the product, and the liner can split in transit.

## Tell us how the drum is filled and emptied

The same drum can need different liners depending on how product goes in and out:

- **Bottom shape** — flat, gusseted, conical or shaped. A conical or shaped bottom helps a drum that is discharged by tipping or from the base.
- **Spout** — if the drum is drawn from repeatedly, an [integrated dispensing spout](/products/round-drum-liners/drum-liner-with-integrated-dispensing-spout) keeps the rest of the batch sealed.
- **Closure** — tied, heat sealed or clipped at the top.
- **Fill temperature** — hot filling decides the film, so give the temperature.

## Then choose the material

Diameter and height make the liner fit; the material makes it work. Polyethylene for general use, EVOH for oxygen, aluminium laminate for moisture, conductive film for static, PVC for aggressive chemicals — the [drum liner materials guide](/blog/drum-liner-materials-guide) compares them.

## Your measurement checklist

- Internal diameter at the top opening
- Internal height, base to rim
- Open-top or tight-head; steel, plastic or fibre
- How it is filled and how it is emptied
- The product, its data sheet and the fill temperature
- Static or barrier requirements
- Approximate annual quantity

If measuring is awkward — an irregular drum, a vessel with internal fittings, a fleet of mixed drums — send one sample container instead and it is measured at the plant. Either way, a trial liner is supplied for fitment before bulk production.
