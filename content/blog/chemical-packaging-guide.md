## Compatibility comes first

In chemical packaging the expensive failure is never the packaging. It is a batch contaminated by a film the product attacked, a powder that took on moisture through a liner that was never a barrier, or a fire at discharge because nobody asked whether the product was flammable.

So the decision runs in a fixed order:

1. **Compatibility** — which film will the product not soften, swell, permeate, discolour or leach from?
2. **Barrier** — does moisture or oxygen spoil the product over the storage and transit time?
3. **Static** — is the product, or the atmosphere around it, ignitable by a static discharge?
4. **Format** — drum, FIBC, container liner, sack or flexi bag, chosen by volume, route and handling.

Get the first three right and the format is a logistics question. Get them wrong and no format will save the batch.

## Step 1: compatibility

| Film | Where it is usually right | Where to be careful |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Polyethylene (PE), multilayer | Most aqueous solutions, many acids and alkalis, salts, polymers, most powders | Some aromatic and chlorinated solvents, oils at temperature — check the data sheet |
| PVC | Products that will not sit safely against PE; where contents must be visible; irregular containers | Plasticiser interaction with some products; temperature limits |
| Aluminium laminate | Moisture- and oxygen-sensitive products; long storage; long voyages | Compatibility is decided by the inner sealing layer, which still has to suit the product |
| EVOH barrier film | Oxygen-sensitive products, oils, resins, flavours | Barrier falls at high humidity unless protected by PE skins, as in a co-extruded structure |
| Conductive film | Flammable powders, solvent-wet products | The conductive layer and the product contact layer both need checking |

Treat the table as a starting point. GI PackTech works compatibility out case by case against the product data sheet before anything is quoted, and declines the job where a film would fail — including where PVC is not the right answer for a product that a buyer assumed needed it.

## Step 2: barrier

A thicker film is not a barrier. Polyethylene of any thickness lets water vapour and oxygen through slowly, which is invisible over a week and decisive over a three-month voyage through humid ports.

- For **hygroscopic powders, catalysts and moisture-sensitive additives**, specify an [aluminium 5- or 6-layer liner](/products/bulk-bag-and-fibc-liners/aluminium-5-and-6-layer-liner). The foil is close to a total barrier; the seal is where the barrier is won or lost.
- For **oxygen-sensitive** products — resins, oils, some intermediates — an [EVOH barrier liner](/products/bulk-bag-and-fibc-liners/evoh-barrier-liner) gives an oxygen barrier with the handling of an ordinary PE liner.

The guide [EVOH vs aluminium barrier liners](/blog/evoh-vs-aluminium-barrier-liners) sets the two side by side.

## Step 3: static

Any powder flowing into or out of a liner generates charge. Whether it matters depends on how easily the product ignites and whether flammable vapour is present. For flammable powders and solvent-wet products, specify [Type C or Type D FIBCs](/blog/fibc-types-a-b-c-d-explained) and a [conductive liner](/products/bulk-bag-and-fibc-liners/conductive-liner) as one system. Where a product needs both static control and a barrier, a [conductive aluminium liner](/products/bulk-bag-and-fibc-liners/conductive-aluminium-liner) does both in one construction instead of double lining.

## Step 4: format

| Format | Typical quantity per unit | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Drum with liner](/products/round-drum-liners) | Up to about 200–250 litres per drum | High-value products, small lots, liquids and pastes, reusable drum programmes |
| [FIBC with liner](/products/bulk-bag-and-fibc-liners) | Several hundred kilograms to over a tonne | Powders and granules moved by forklift into a process |
| [Container liner](/products/container-liners-and-sacks/container-liner-pp-woven-and-pe-film) | A full 20 or 40 foot container, loose | Single-product dry bulk exports at volume |
| [25 or 50 kg sack](/products/container-liners-and-sacks/heavy-duty-bulk-bags-25kg-and-50kg) | 25 or 50 kg | Products sold through dealers or handled by hand |
| [Liquid flexi bag](/products/liquid-flexi-bags-and-bladder-tanks/liquid-flexi-bag) | 1,000–1,500 litres | Non-hazardous liquids without an IBC to clean and return |

On the plant floor itself, a flexible [spill containment bund](/products/hazardous-waste-containment/spill-containment-bund-and-drip-tray) under drum and IBC storage is often required by the site's environmental permit and takes minutes to place.

## Dangerous goods

If the product is classified as dangerous goods for transport, the packaging as a whole must be of an approved design type under the UN Recommendations on the Transport of Dangerous Goods — the drum or FIBC design, tested and marked. The liner forms part of that design. Give the supplier the UN number, the packing group and the approved packaging you use, and the liner is specified within it rather than as a substitute for it.

## What to send

- The product data sheet, and the safety data sheet if the product is hazardous
- Container type and measured internal dimensions
- Fill temperature, storage temperature and the expected storage and transit time
- Whether flammable vapour or solvent is present at filling or discharge
- Annual quantity, destination and handling method
