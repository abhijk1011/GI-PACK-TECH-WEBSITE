## Two barriers for two problems

When a product spoils in its packaging, the cause is almost always one of two things getting in: **oxygen** or **moisture**. They are stopped by different materials.

- Oxygen causes oxidation — colour drift, rancidity in oils and fats, fading aroma in flavours, loss of activity in some chemicals.
- Moisture causes caking, clumping, hydrolysis, loss of catalyst activity and nutrient loss in fertilisers.

Ordinary polyethylene stops neither for long. It is a good moisture barrier compared with paper and a poor oxygen barrier, and over weeks or months the product inside equalises with the air outside. The two high-barrier options are EVOH and aluminium foil laminate.

## EVOH: the oxygen barrier that handles like PE

EVOH (ethylene vinyl alcohol copolymer) has one of the lowest oxygen transmission rates of any common packaging polymer — orders of magnitude lower than polyethylene. Its weakness is water: EVOH absorbs moisture, and its oxygen barrier falls as it does.

So EVOH is always used as a thin layer **co-extruded between polyethylene skins**. The polyethylene keeps moisture away from the EVOH; the EVOH stops the oxygen. The result is an [EVOH barrier liner](/products/bulk-bag-and-fibc-liners/evoh-barrier-liner) that seals, flexes and handles like an ordinary PE liner while giving a strong oxygen barrier.

## Aluminium foil laminate: near-total barrier

An aluminium laminate puts a layer of aluminium foil between polymer layers — typically polyester for strength and print, nylon for puncture resistance, and polyethylene as the sealing layer. Intact foil is effectively impermeable to water vapour, oxygen and other gases, and it blocks light.

[Aluminium 5- and 6-layer liners](/products/bulk-bag-and-fibc-liners/aluminium-5-and-6-layer-liner) are specified where the product is high value, the storage period is long, or the route passes through long spells of heat and humidity. The six-layer construction adds margin for the hardest routes.

### Where aluminium fails

Because the foil itself stops almost everything, the paths that remain are:

- **The heat seal**, where the foil is interrupted and the sealing layer carries the barrier
- **Flex cracks and pinholes** from folding, filling and rough handling
- **Punctures** from sharp product or careless handling

That is why GI PackTech's development work on barrier liners has gone into the seal. A barrier film is only as good as its weakest seal, and most barrier failures happen there rather than through the film.

## How to choose

1. **Which spoils the product — oxygen, moisture, or both?** Oxygen only → EVOH. Moisture, or both → aluminium.
2. **How long, and through what climate?** Long storage and humid routes favour aluminium.
3. **Is light a factor?** If the product is light sensitive, aluminium.
4. **How is it handled?** Heavy handling and repeated folding favour EVOH's flexibility, or an aluminium laminate built for it.
5. **Does it also need shape or static control?** EVOH can be built into [baffle](/products/bulk-bag-and-fibc-liners/baffle-and-gambo-liner) and box type liners; aluminium can be combined with a conductive layer.

## Prove it with a trial

The most reliable way to decide is a side-by-side trial: one batch in your current liner, one in the barrier liner, checked at the same interval for the property that matters — moisture content, peroxide value, colour, activity.
